# Media keys and media mode

Area: media keys and media mode.
READMEs: `src/content/media/README.md`, `src/content/quality/README.md`, `src/background/README.md`.
Source: `src/content/media`, `src/content/quality`, `src/background`.

### Media keys and media mode

#### Keys and actions

- The `a` group is Media. `am` mutes the tab (`tab.mute`, also `<A-m>`).
- The rest run `media` (`media.act`) with an action: `ap` play or pause, `as` and `aS` speed up and down by 0.05x (0.25x to 4x), `ar` speed back to 1x, `av` and `aV` volume up and down by 5 points, `al` and `ah` seek 5 s forward and back, `aP` picture-in-picture on or off, `af` fullscreen (`src/content/media/fullscreen.ts`): it clicks the site player's own fullscreen button when one inside the player box carries a label, title or class that names fullscreen, relying on the key press's activation.
- Otherwise it requests fullscreen on the player box (the same box as the `;F` hint, `src/content/player_box.ts`, so the site's controls stay). A fixed pixel height inside the box can then leave the video short of full screen, because Firefox allows one fullscreen request per key press (see [firefox-behavior.md](../firefox-behavior.md), "Fullscreen requests per key press"). The key again leaves every nested level, since `exitFullscreen` leaves one.
- `media mute`, `media loop`, `media info`, `media quality` and `media skip` have no key. `mute` and `loop` toggle the element (the tab stays unmuted), and `info` shows the time, speed, quality and volume. `A` opens the line with `media ` typed.
- On the line the value of `speed`, `volume` and `seek` is `up`, `down`, `reset` (1x, 100% or the start), `end` (the top of the range, the media end for `seek`), `percent` (the count as a percent of the range, so `50` goes to the middle of the media and `volume` to 50%), a signed number that moves by it (`media seek -10`) or a bare number that sets it (`media speed 1.5`, `media volume 60`).
- Each action takes its own values, as `pattern_arms` keyed on `action`, so the schema refuses `media speed end`, `media volume percent` and any value on an action that takes none. `percent` is for `seek` only, and it needs its count: `media seek percent` with no count is refused as `missing <times>`. A value outside the range is set to the nearest end, not refused: speed stays within 0.25x to 4x, volume within 0 to 100 and seek within the media.
- Without a value the notice shows the current one and nothing is written. A count fills `times`, which the schema allows only with `up`, `down` or `percent`, so `3as` moves three steps in one press and `2ap` is refused. Speed is not kept: a new video starts at its own rate.

#### Quality, skip, captions and frame step

- `media quality` and `media skip` reach the site's own player, not the media element, because a video element has no quality control. `quality.js` (`src/content/quality/`) holds one driver per site player and no host list: each driver probes the page for its handle.
- The background injects the bundle into every frame, each frame reports whether it has a player and whether the player lists its levels, and the background sends the one `quality_apply` request to a single frame: one that lists levels first, then the top frame, then the lowest frame id. So a page with several embeds changes or skips one player. A frame that refuses the script does not stop the others, and the whole request ends after 1.2 s without sending anything late.
- The bundle reaches the handle through `window.wrappedJSObject`, the way `hints/inspect.ts` does, so a site CSP does not matter and no script is injected into the page. The drivers use player calls only and write no cookie or storage.
- The YouTube driver reads the `#movie_player` element's unofficial calls (`getAvailableQualityData`, `setPlaybackQualityRange`, `nextVideo`, `previousVideo`), which can break when YouTube changes them, and the player answers "unknown" until it plays, so a request before that refuses with "The player has no quality levels yet".
- The hls.js driver reads `html5player.hlsobj`, the hls.js instance of the site's player: `levels`, and `loadLevel` to read the level and `nextLevel` only to switch (`-1` is auto), because `currentLevel` reads -1 until a fragment is buffered and a write to it flushes the buffer. A pure chooser (`choose_level`) turns `max`, `min`, a height (the highest level at or below it, else the lowest) and `up` or `down` (the count in steps, from the playing level) into a level, so the drivers hold no policy.
- `auto` hands the choice back to the site. With no value the notice lists the levels, best first, with the playing one marked. The driver reads the page again for each request, so a player that YouTube replaces on in-site navigation is found again. Where no driver probes, the notice says "No quality control on this page". `skip` takes `next` (the default) or `previous`, and refuses with "No next video" where the player offers none, as the hls.js driver does.
- `media captions` (`c` in the media mode) reads `video.textTracks` first in the `media` bundle: any showing subtitle or caption track goes to `disabled`, else the default track (or the first) goes to `showing`, and `hidden` counts as off, because a site that draws its own captions keeps its tracks hidden. With no such track the background asks the quality bundle, and the YouTube driver calls the player's unofficial `toggleSubtitles`, guarded so a player without it refuses. Else the notice says "No captions".
- The hls.js driver has no captions call, since its tracks reach `textTracks`, and the site may reset a mode that the key set. `media frame up|down` (`.` and `,`) pauses the video and moves it by the count of thirtieths of a second within 0 to the duration. No API gives the frame rate, so a 24 or 60 fps video steps by a guess. Binding `c`, `,` and `.` takes them from the page in the media mode. On the command line, `media quality ` completes `max min auto up down` and the levels the player offers when the line opens.
- The line asks the background the first time it reads the list, and keeps the answer until it closes, so the list is as old as the line. Each row is named by the height the schema takes, with the site label (such as 1080p60) in the description.

#### Which media a key acts on

- A media key acts on every video or audio that plays with sound (not muted, volume above 0) in every frame, else on every one that plays, else on the largest visible video, else on the first media element. Size beats playing: a video that plays, or plays with sound, counts only when its visible area is at least a quarter of the largest visible video's area (`PLAYING_MIN_SHARE`). Otherwise the largest visible video acts, so a small ad that plays does not outrank the main video that waits to start.
- A frame with no video always counts, but a hidden or off-screen video does not. Inside one frame, an audible or playing target takes every playing element of that frame, and fullscreen and picture-in-picture take the largest. So a muted autoplay preview yields to the video the user hears. `ap` is the exception: with sound playing anywhere, it pauses every playing element on the tab, a muted preview too, so the next `ap` finds nothing playing and resumes the largest visible video.
- Picture-in-picture and fullscreen take one video: the open one (to close it), else the largest with sound, else the largest playing one (each only when big enough, as above), else the largest visible one. The code runs in the background (`src/background/media_actions.ts`), because the player often sits in a frame other than the one that took the key.
- The background lists the tab's frames with `webNavigation.getAllFrames` and asks each one for its scan by frame id (`media_scan`), each ask cut off after 300 ms, so a frame that hangs holds the key for 300 ms and no longer. The list holds every frame the tab has. It then picks the frames and asks those to apply. A frame that ran `media.js` for an earlier key still has its listener, so a later key injects nothing.
- A frame that rejects the ask or answers something else has no listener: its document is new (the first key on a page, a frame created or navigated since) or only `content.js` runs there. The background injects `media.js` into those frames by `frameId` (`runAt: document_start`, each call cut off after 300 ms) and asks only them again. A frame that times out is left alone: injecting a hung frame would add a second wait. So a slow frame that misses the 300 ms is not injected on that key and counts as one with no media. A sandbox without `allow-scripts` refuses the script (measured, see [firefox-behavior.md](../firefox-behavior.md), "Media keys across frames"), and so does a privileged page. `about:blank` and `srcdoc` frames are reached. A `data:` frame is not: its player is out of reach by any call, and a page whose only video sits there refuses with "No video or audio on this page" (measured in the same section). A frame that refuses costs two quick failed calls per key, and one whose injection hangs adds up to 300 ms. If the frame list call fails or takes over 300 ms, the top frame is the one frame asked. The scans are sorted by frame id, so ties go to the lowest id whatever order `getAllFrames` lists. The `webNavigation` permission is the cost: it very likely adds an install prompt line and makes Firefox ask users again on update (not measured). It reads the frame list and nothing else, and the extension already holds `tabs` and `<all_urls>`.
- A frame finds its media again at apply time, through closed shadow roots (`openOrClosedShadowRoot`), so it never acts on elements the page replaced after the scan. A frame that refuses the script, never answers a scan within 300 ms, or answers the apply with no result (it navigated or closed) counts as one with no media, and an answer that comes after its wait is dropped, so a late frame never acts twice. Each answer returns to the request that asked, so two keys pressed fast never read each other's scans. The content script's own listener answers nothing to the `media_apply` message, so the bundle's answer is the reply, and `content.js` grows by nothing.
- A second injection into a frame adds no second listener. One info notice in the frame that took the key says the result ("Speed 1.05x", "Volume 60%", "1:25 of 3:40", "Speed 1.1x, 2 players"). Failures are refusals: "No video or audio on this page", a blocked `play()` and the picture-in-picture errors in words. A `play()` still pending after 300 ms counts as started, because a buffering source resolves it only once playback runs, and the background stops waiting for a frame's apply after 1.5 s.
- The key's activation reaches `play()` and picture-in-picture through the background round trip, except for picture-in-picture in a frame from another origin (see [firefox-behavior.md](../firefox-behavior.md), "Media keys across frames").

- Known limits of the media keys: a muted preview counts as playing when nothing plays with sound. A frame scrolled out of its parent's view still reports its own visible area, which matters only when nothing plays. With several targets at different rates, each moves from its own rate and the notice shows the first. Two quick `ap` presses both see the same playing media, so the second does not toggle back. A hidden `<audio>` sound effect can be the first media element on a page with no video.

#### Media mode

- The media mode is a real mode with its own keymap section `[media]`, and the tab holds it, not one frame. `aa` (`media enter`), the hint `;M` and any element fullscreen start it, and `mode.set` cannot, so a page that is not fullscreen and a frame under an all-off rule stay out of it. Only normal mode enters it.
- A `fullscreenchange` listener on the document of each frame that runs the content script does the fullscreen part, whoever started the fullscreen: it enters media from normal when an element goes fullscreen and returns to normal when the fullscreen ends in media (`fullscreen_step` in `mode_state.ts`).
- Outside full screen the badge says MEDIA and the which-key panel of the mode opens by itself when the top frame enters or returns to it (`create_media_discovery` in `tab_media.ts` asks frame 0 once per move into the mode, never for a focus report, unless `which_key_delay_ms` is 0. The frame draws only while it is still in media mode and not in full screen, and a key that runs in a child frame in the mode closes the panel from the background, because child keys never reach the top frame).
- In full screen the badge hides and no panel opens, and a `fullscreenchange` refreshes the badge and closes the panel. `?` opens the panel by hand. Keys: `F` fullscreen, `h` `l` seek (`5l` repeats, `0` the start, `$` the end, `N%` goes to N percent), `H` `L` previous and next video, `j` `k` volume, `<` `>` `=` speed, `m` mute, Space play or pause, `p` picture-in-picture, `q` opens the line with `media quality `, `r` loop, `i` info, `c` captions, `,` and `.` the previous and next frame.
- `f`, `;` (with its modifiers) and `:` are the ones of normal mode. Every other key goes to the page, so a site player keeps its shortcuts, except `/` and `'`, which the mode cancels as normal mode does so Quick Find never starts. Normal mode's other keys (`j` scrolling) are out of reach. Digits are counts, as in normal mode. A key acts on the pinned video, else on the main video by the rule above.
- The pin is a weak reference that `;M` leaves on `window` in the frame of the video, and the background keeps the pinned frame; a video that leaves the page (a pre-roll ad swapped for the main video) hands the keys back to the main video rule. A hint (`f`, `;`) or the command line (`:`) opened from the mode returns to it, by Escape or after a pick or a command.
- The mode ends on Escape (which also calls `exitFullscreen`, and returns to item mode when the mode began there with `aa` or `;M` from item mode), when the fullscreen ends, when a field takes the focus (insert mode), and when a new page loads (`tabs.onUpdated` with `status: "loading"` and no `url`). An address change the page makes itself (`pushState` or `replaceState`, also one before the load event: YouTube's next video, autoplay) keeps the mode and drops the pin, so the keys act on the new main video. Entering the mode ends an active site mode.
- It is tab-wide: the background (`tab_media.ts`) keeps the frames that reported media in `storage.session`. The first frame to report media starts the mode in every frame of the tab that has started its content script: the background sends a media step to all of them, and each takes the step a fullscreen change takes, so a frame in ignore, insert or any mode but normal keeps its mode. `aa` sends that step to every frame itself, the frame that took the key among them, and the first report then sends none.
- A second step on could reach the key's frame after an Escape there and put it back in the mode. A frame that a step or a join reply put in the mode says so in its report (`step`). Such a report never starts a hold: with no live hold, the step came from a hold that ended before the frame took it (a site key that enters and leaves fullscreen at once, `aa` and a fast Escape), so the background sends that frame the step off.
- Without this, its report started a new hold, whose step on put every other frame back in the mode. With a live hold, the frame joins it. The frame that sees the end reports its new mode as every frame does, and the background sends the media step off to the other frames of the hold, never back to the reporter. A frame of the hold with a hint or the line open keeps it, and closing it lands in normal mode. A report of hint or command line mode keeps the hold. `aa` needs a video, not only audio.
- `;M` pins only a video (never audio) of its own frame and refuses one in a same-origin frame. `q`, `H` and `L` ask the player in the pinned frame first, while the pinned video is on the page. A fullscreen that ends in a frame while a hint or the line is open there, opened from the mode, closes it and keeps the frame in the mode, because Firefox spends the Escape on leaving fullscreen (see [firefox-behavior.md](../firefox-behavior.md), "Escape in fullscreen").
- The end reaches every frame of the fullscreen, so with the player in a child frame the frames without the line report normal, in any order with the line's close. The background keeps the hold while a frame of the hold has a hint or the line open that it opened from the mode, and sends the media step back to each frame that reported normal meanwhile once that frame closes it.
- A normal report within 500 ms after such a line closed back to the mode (`LINE_CLOSE_GRACE_MS`), from a frame without the focus, is the same end and goes back to the mode too. An Escape the user presses comes from the frame with the focus, so it still ends the mode. A line frame that no longer answers a ping keeps nothing. When the mode ends in another frame, closing the line lands in normal mode. A `%` key with no count refuses with "Type a number first".
- A frame that starts its content script on its first focus after the mode began joins it, when it starts in normal mode with no editable focus. Its first keymap read says so (`join`) and the reply carries `join: "media"` (`tab_media.join`: true when the hold has frames or `aa` stepped on, a pin alone does not count). The background writes the frame into the hold in the same turn, so an end after the read sends the frame the step off, and its report finds it in the hold.
- A step off that reaches the frame before it applies the reply is lost, and the rule that a stepped report (`step`) of media mode with no live hold gets the step off sends that frame back to normal. Insert wins over media. A frame under an all-off rule never joins, and a re-ask of the keymap (config reload, macro recording) never joins.

### Background files for media

Behavior of the `src/background` files for this area, moved here from `src/background/README.md`.

- `tab_media.ts`: the media mode of a tab, in `storage.session`: the frames that reported it and the frame whose video `;M` pinned. The first report of media starts it in every frame of the tab (a `MediaStep` on). A frame of the hold that reports a mode other than media, hint or command line ends it for every frame (a `MediaStep` off to the others), except a normal report while another frame of the hold has a hint or the line open that it opened from the mode, or just after such a line closed (the end of a fullscreen), which keeps the hold. A new page load ends it too. An address change the page makes keeps it and drops the pin. `media enter` starts it. `join` lets a frame that starts late (first focus) into the hold, through the reply to its first keymap read.
- `tab_steps.ts`: the mode steps of a tab go out in order, and the shared queue of reports never waits on them. Media and item mode share one queue, because a frame in one mode takes the other mode's step. A step to one frame waits only for the earlier steps that can reach that frame, and a step to every frame waits for all earlier steps, so a frame that never answers holds up no step to another frame. A new step drops the waiting steps of its mode that it covers, a frame that reports the mode on its own or whose new document joins drops those waiting for it alone, and a load or a close drops the steps of both modes of the tab at once (`reset`), so the clear of one mode never sets a waiting step of the other free. A hold stores the document each frame joined from, and a step to a frame names it, so a step for a document that another one replaced never lands. A frame that starts a hold on its own while an off step of an older hold is on its way to it is marked when that off is for the same document, and its next report of normal, which is that off landing, steps it back on and keeps the hold.
- `media_actions.ts`: the `media` command across frames. It lists the frames (`webNavigation.getAllFrames`), asks each to scan by frame id, injects `media.js` by frame id into the frames that have no listener (never a frame that timed out) and asks those again, picks the frames that act (`choose_frames`: every frame with media that plays with sound, else with any playing media, else the largest visible video) and words the one notice or refusal. A frame that refuses the script, times out after 300 ms or answers nothing counts as one without media. `quality` and `skip` skip the scan: each frame reports its player and the request goes to one chosen frame. Browser calls come in through `MediaDeps`.
- `media_quality.ts`: the `media_quality` rule. `tab_updated` turns a changed address (a navigation) or a finished load into one injection of the probe (`quality_probe.js`) into every frame, and a second pass 1 s later for `about:blank` frames. `probe_reported` injects `quality.js` into the one frame whose probe found a video, unless that frame has left the address the probe saw. It injects nothing when `is_idle` says the extension is off or the rule is `off` or `auto` everywhere with no site row asks for a quality (`media_quality_is_idle` in `page_keymap.ts`). The newest update of a tab decides, and an earlier one that is superseded injects nothing more. `rule_for_report` answers a frame's `quality_report`: the rule for its address through `media_quality_reply` in `page_keymap.ts` when the frame has a video, nothing otherwise. The listener in `main.ts` filters `tabs.onUpdated` to `url` and `status`.
- `quality_levels.ts`: the `quality` bundle seen from the background, and the injections of the probe and of the bundle into one frame. `ask_quality` injects `quality.js`, collects each frame's report, picks one player (`choose_player`) and sends the request to that frame alone, and `quality_rows` makes the completion rows of `media quality ` (the fixed words, then the levels).
